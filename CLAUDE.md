# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Layout

- `forma/` is the actual project: a single-page landing site for **Forma**, an architecture design firm, built with Next.js 16 (App Router), React 19, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS v4. It is its own git repo.
- `test.txt` at the root is a scratch file and has nothing to do with the site.
- `README.md` at the root (Portuguese) is the public face of the repo on GitHub; keep its numbers in sync with the case study.

The owner directs the build and reviews every change; explain changes in plain language.

## Business context

### What Forma does
Forma is a **solo architecture practice** designing calm, material-led homes, small workspaces, interiors and landscapes. **Residential architecture is the main focus.** The client works personally with one architect, from understanding the site and drawing the first sketch through construction and handover.

### Who the website is for
- **Primary visitor:** someone with a plot of land, an existing house or an idea for a home, looking for an architect to develop it.
- **Secondary visitors:** people who need help with a small workplace, an interior or the landscape around a building.

Visitors need to understand Forma's approach, decide whether it fits their project, and feel comfortable starting a conversation.

### Why someone would choose Forma
- **One architect throughout.** The person who understands the brief also develops the design and stays involved during construction. Direct access and continuity are central to the offer.
- **The site comes first.** Sunlight, views, neighbours, ground conditions and how the client lives shape the design.
- **Fewer, better materials.** Timber, lime render, stone and steel, in a restrained palette meant to age well and need little upkeep.
- **Attention to each project.** Forma takes on fewer projects and stays personally involved.

### What the website needs to achieve
Turn interested visitors into **project enquiries**.
- **Primary CTA:** "Start a project", which leads to the contact form.
- **Secondary CTA:** "About the practice", so visitors know who they'd work with before enquiring.
- **The contact form asks for:** name, email, project type, and a message about what they want to build.

## Next.js version caveat

`forma/AGENTS.md` (imported by `forma/CLAUDE.md`, and regenerated by `next dev`) warns that this Next.js version differs from older ones. Read the bundled docs in `forma/node_modules/next/dist/docs/` before using a Next API. Known differences that affect this code:
- `next/image` uses `preload`; `priority` is deprecated.
- `images.qualities` defaults to `[75]`, so other `quality` values are coerced.
- Remote images must be allowed in `images.remotePatterns` in `next.config.ts`. None are allowed now: every image is local.

## Architecture

- **Content is separate from presentation.** All copy, images, links and lists live in `forma/src/data/content.ts`. Components import from it and contain no hardcoded copy. New content goes in `content.ts`, never inline in a component.
- **Portfolio concept:** the site is a concept piece for the owner's portfolio. Forma, "Helena Duarte" and the projects are fictional; every image and video is AI-generated. The page says so (`concept` in `content.ts`: a note under Selected work and in the footer). The About image is a crop of the clay studio scene (`public/images/studio.jpg`), not a real person. Never add numbers, client quotes, logos or awards that the owner hasn't supplied. The design rules ban invented proof.
- **Case study:** `/how-it-was-built` (`src/app/how-it-was-built/page.tsx`, copy in `howItWasBuilt` in `content.ts`, in Portuguese) explains the build. Every figure on it was checked against the repo; re-check before changing one.
- **Primary CTA:** every "Start a project" link points to `#contact`. The pinned top bar comes from the scroll-world engine (`cta` in `ScrollWorld.tsx`).
- **Scroll motion (owner's request):** the portrait and the Selected work images rise into view once (opening upward while fading in from a soft blur, about 1 s) the first time they scroll into view (`components/Reveal.tsx`, `.reveal` in `globals.css`). The text of About, What I design and How a project unfolds is written out word by word as the visitor scrolls (`components/WriteOn.tsx` around the block, copy wrapped in `<Words text=… />`, `[data-write]` in `globals.css`). Both skip motion under reduced motion and hide nothing without JS. Keep other motion sparse and discuss before adding more.
- **Contact form (demo):** `components/ContactForm.tsx` validates on blur and on submit, then shows a demo confirmation (`contact.sent`); nothing is sent or stored, and there is no contact email on the page. A real form service would replace that step in `handleSubmit`.
- **AI brief:** "Organise my brief with AI" in the form posts the message to `src/app/api/brief/route.ts`, which calls Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`, effort `low`, structured output via `betaZodOutputFormat`, `fallbacks: "default"`) and returns a brief the form shows in a card (copy in `brief` in `content.ts`). It needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (in `forma/.env.local` locally, and as a Vercel environment variable in production); without it the route returns 503 and the form says the AI is switched off. Input is capped at 1,500 characters, with a best-effort in-memory limit of 5 briefs per visitor per 10 minutes. The visitor's text goes inside `<enquiry>` tags and is treated as data.
- **Page order:** `src/app/page.tsx` composes the sections: ScrollWorld (the walkthrough, `#top`; its first scene carries the letter's greeting and title) → About (`#about`) → Projects (`#work`) → Services (`#services`) → Process → Contact (`#contact`) → Footer. The old Header and Hero components were removed.
- **Link preview:** `src/app/opengraph-image.jpg` (+ `.alt.txt`), a crop of the home island; `metadataBase` in `layout.tsx` points at the production URL.

## Design system (Hallmark)

The page was redesigned with the Hallmark skill (`.agents/skills/hallmark`, also linked as `.claude/skills/hallmark`). Its choices are recorded in the stamp comment at the top of `forma/src/app/globals.css` and in `forma/.hallmark/log.json`. Read both before changing the design.

- **Shape:** the page uses the "Letter" layout. It reads as a first-person note from the architect, with an austere tone and very little motion (only the scroll reveals). The nav is just the wordmark and one CTA (no link row), and the footer closes like a letter.
- **Visual system:** colours, fonts and corner radii come from `Design.md` at the repo root (a "Havn" template system; the owner switched it to light mode). The page structure, layout and motion are Forma's own and must stay as they are when that file changes; only the visual layer follows it.
- **Tokens:** all tokens live in `forma/tokens.css` as a Tailwind v4 `@theme static` block, imported by `globals.css`. They become both CSS variables and utilities:
  - **Colours** (role names kept, light mode): `paper` #FAFAF9 (background), `paper-2` #F4F4F5 (surface), `rule` #E4E4E7 (border), `muted` #52525B, `ink` #0A0A0A, `accent` #EAB308 for fills (buttons, dots) with `accent-hover` and `accent-ink` (black text on yellow), `accent-text` #854D0E wherever the accent is used as text (yellow text on white fails contrast), plus `focus`, `error` and `success`
  - **Fonts:** `font-display` (DM Serif Display, for headings and short display lines), `font-body` (Inter) and `font-mono` (JetBrains Mono, for labels and metadata, paired with `text-label`)
  - **Sizes:** `text-label`, `text-sm`, `text-base`, `text-md`, `text-xl`, `text-display`
  - **Radii:** `rounded-card` (16px) for images, `rounded-control` (8px) for form fields, `rounded-pill` for buttons
  - **Spacing:** named steps `2xs` to `4xl` (`p-md`, `gap-xl`, …)
- **Never redefine a token under `@theme inline`** (for example `--color-paper: var(--color-paper)`). Tailwind emits a self-referencing variable that breaks the colour.
- **Spacing names collide with width utilities.** Because `--spacing-sm` … `--spacing-4xl` exist, `max-w-sm`, `max-w-2xl` and similar resolve to the spacing scale (for example `max-w-2xl` becomes 4rem). Use explicit widths like `max-w-[42rem]` or `max-w-[60ch]` instead.
- **Rules the design follows:**
  - headings are never italic
  - no section eyebrow labels
  - colours only come from tokens
  - clickable text never wraps (`whitespace-nowrap`)
  - grids that contain images use `minmax(0, 1fr)` tracks
  - every interactive element has hover, focus-visible and active states, and form fields keep a 1px border in every state

## `scroll-world` branch

This branch opens the page with a scroll-scrubbed camera walkthrough, built with the scroll-world skill. It's architecture A: one forward leg per scene, with no connectors.
- **Engine:** vendored unmodified at `forma/public/world/scrub-engine.js` (excluded from ESLint). It's mounted once by `components/ScrollWorld.tsx` and themed through unlayered `.sw-*` overrides at the end of `globals.css`.
- **Config and copy:** `world` in `content.ts`.
- **Page structure:** the rest of the page sits in a `relative z-[70]` wrapper, so it slides over the engine's fixed layers. The engine assumes its container is at the very top of the page (scroll positions start at 0), so nothing may go above `<ScrollWorld />`.
- **Footage is free placeholder material:** camera push-ins on 3D clay-island scene images. `bash world/prepare-sources.sh` builds `forma/world/sources/<id>.jpg` from the originals in `forma/photos/`: it removes the grey background with `world/knockout.py` (needs Pillow) and places the island right of centre on a `#FEFDFD` canvas. That colour must match `--sw-bg` in `globals.css`, or a box edge shows. `bash world/build-placeholder.sh` (run in `forma/`, needs ffmpeg) rebuilds `public/world/`. Each leg opens on the previous leg's real last frame, so the seams are frame-identical. Read the last frame with `-update 1` and without `-frames:v 1`, otherwise you get a frame a few frames early.
- **Real AI footage (live on this branch):** 5 island dives + 4 connector flights, 480p, rendered through Monid (Seedance 2.0) by `world/render-world.sh` (paid; `one <name>` re-renders a single clip) and encoded into `public/world/ai/`. `world` in `content.ts` points there. Phones get lighter copies from `public/world/ai/m/` (`clipMobile` / `connectorsMobile`, built for free by `bash world/encode-mobile.sh`); rerun it whenever the AI clips change. The free push-ins in `public/world/vid/` are the fallback. Costs and known quirks are in `forma/world/brief.md`. Never run the paid script without the owner's go-ahead.
